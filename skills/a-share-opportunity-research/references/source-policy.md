# Source Policy

## Source ladder

### Tier A — Primary / official
Use for factual confirmation and company exposure:
- exchange filings and company filings;
- government and regulator data;
- customs and public tenders;
- standards bodies and industry associations with original data;
- customer/supplier official announcements.

### Tier B — Professional data
Use for corroboration and magnitude:
- industry trackers with visible methodology;
- trade publications with named sources;
- earnings-call transcripts;
- reputable reporting based on direct fieldwork/interviews.

### Tier C — Practitioner firsthand
Use for early discovery:
- procurement, installation, engineering, operations, channel and customer discussions where the author appears to have direct operational exposure.

### Tier D — Specialist community
Use for discovery and technical interpretation when identity/firsthand status is incomplete.

### Tier E — Investor social
雪球、股吧, general stock forums and similar sources are useful mainly for:
- attention;
- narrative saturation;
- expectations;
- discovery.

They do not independently establish factual truth.

### Tier F — Repost / aggregator
No independent corroboration credit.

## Required checks

For every evidence item record:
- URL;
- published time;
- first-available time if different;
- source class;
- identity/role if known;
- firsthand versus hearsay;
- specificity;
- independence group;
- promotional/ticker-pitch flags;
- edited-after-cutoff flag if known;
- relation: supports / contradicts / context.

## Time discipline

Historical selection may use only evidence available by the cutoff.

When exact intraday time is unknown, use a conservative availability assumption. Do not move a source earlier simply because it helps the thesis.

## Corroboration discipline

Ten articles copying the same underlying report count as one evidence root.

Search specifically for evidence that would contradict the preferred story.
