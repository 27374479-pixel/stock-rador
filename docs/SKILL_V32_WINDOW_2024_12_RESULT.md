## Learning classification

This is the first clean post-promotion test of the V3.2 causal-horizon requirement.

The pre-outcome Skill did **not** classify the transformer shortage as a short commodity repricing event. It explicitly expected a multi-quarter/multi-year order-and-capacity mechanism and chose a 120–250 trading-day research horizon.

The 250-day result strongly supports keeping the distinction between:
- short-lived event repricing;
- multi-quarter cyclicals;
- structural multi-year earnings transitions.

It also shows why a mechanical rule such as “exit when 120-day excess turns negative” would have destroyed a successful structural thesis.

The correct forward question is not “how many days should we hold every stock?” It is:

> Is the original causal mechanism still intact, and are the predeclared normalization indicators actually triggering?

For 伊戈尔, the predeclared normalization indicators were transformer lead-time compression, broad North-American capacity additions, slowing North-American order growth, or delayed overseas qualification/ramp. Holding-horizon logic should respond to those causal variables rather than a fixed calendar day.
